Responsible use and privacy#

Interview App is built to help you prepare for interviews, take transparent notes in meetings, and run a focused, honest job search. It is deliberately not a tool for getting covert help during a live interview, and not a tool for mass-applying on your behalf.

What it does#

  • Practice coaching. It generates questions from your resume and a job description, runs spoken mock interviews, gives live structure and delivery hints inside practice sessions, scores your answers and tracks your progress.
  • Transparent meeting notes. It transcribes meetings you have consent to record, in a normal visible window with a recording indicator. During the meeting it can search what was said and give you a recap. After the meeting it writes enhanced notes, answers questions with citations and drafts follow-ups.
  • Interview debriefs. After a real interview, it captures your own recap and turns the questions you were asked into practice material.
  • Job search with you in charge. It reads open roles from company job boards through their official public APIs, ranks them against your CV, and drafts a tailored cover letter and answers from your CV. A fact-checker flags every claim your CV doesn't support. You review, edit and approve each application. If you want, a browser assistant then types your approved answers into the employer's own form. You press Submit.

What it deliberately does not do#

  • It doesn't hide from screen sharing, recording, proctoring software or the task bar. There is no stealth mode.
  • It doesn't generate answers for you to say during a live meeting or interview. Live meeting tools only search or summarise what was already said. AI Q&A over a meeting unlocks once the meeting is saved.
  • It doesn't join calls as a bot or capture audio without your action. You start each audio source yourself, and the browser shows its own sharing indicator.
  • It doesn't store audio or build voiceprints. Only text is kept.

Using AI assistance in a live interview without the interviewer's knowledge misrepresents your abilities. It also usually breaks the hiring process's rules. Practise with the coach, then interview as yourself.

Job applications: how the app keeps them honest and yours#

Recruiters now receive a flood of AI-generated applications. They respond with application limits, AI-detection flags and identity checks, and AI-written letters that all sound alike carry less weight than they used to. Fewer, better-matched, truthful applications work better. The Jobs module is built around that:

  • You approve every application, and you submit it. The app never sends anything to an employer by itself. The browser assistant fills the form and stops before Submit. A guard blocks form submissions while it types. After you approve, any change sends the application back for review.
  • A daily limit (10 approvals by default, adjustable in Settings) keeps the search focused.
  • Grounded drafts. Drafts use only your CV and the facts you confirm. A rule-based checker plus an AI fact-checker flag invented numbers, employers, degrees, certifications, tools and inflated experience. You must fix or confirm each blocking claim before you can approve.
  • Questions the AI never answers:
  • Work authorisation, visa sponsorship, criminal record, relatives, previous employment: you answer. Your answers on sponsorship and authorisation are remembered for jobs in the same country only.
  • Salary history: you answer. Your salary expectation comes only from what you typed.
  • Consents, arbitration agreements, recording consent, AI-policy acknowledgements and truthfulness attestations: you tick them yourself in the form.
  • Voluntary self-identification (gender, race, veteran and disability status): never filled and never stored by the app. Answering is optional, and "decline to self-identify" is always an option.
  • The employer's AI policy. If a posting contains instructions aimed at AI tools, the app shows them to you and the draft ignores them. Read them and decide. Where an employer screens with AI and offers a human review (for example Greenhouse's opt-out link), the app shows that link.
  • Official sources only. Jobs come from the public job-board APIs that company career pages use (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters) and from aggregators that offer a public API under their terms (Remotive, with attribution and a link back; Arbeitnow). The app does not scrape or automate LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri or Glassdoor; their terms forbid it. Paste a job from those sites by hand, and the app helps you prepare it.
  • No CAPTCHA solving, no logins, no account creation. If a form needs any of that, the assistant stops and hands the page to you.
  • Fairness. Before any model reads your CV, the app removes your name, contact details and personal attributes (date of birth, gender, marital status, nationality, religion, address). It never infers nationality, visa status or gender. It gives no salary advice: it shows the posted pay range when there is one.

Recording consent (not legal advice)#

Laws differ by place, and calls often span several jurisdictions. The app asks you to confirm consent before every meeting and gives you a disclosure line to paste into the meeting chat.

  • United States: federal law and most states require one party's consent. About a dozen states require all parties' consent, including California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. When participants are in different states, follow the strictest rule.
  • EU / EEA (GDPR): recording is processing of personal data. You need a lawful basis and must tell people before recording. Some countries criminalise secret recording.
  • United Kingdom: a participant recording for their own use is generally lawful. Organisations need a lawful basis and must make reasonable efforts to inform people.
  • India: there is no single statute. Courts have treated secret recording as a privacy violation in several cases, so get consent.

Practical rule: tell everyone at the start ("I'm using a note-taking app that transcribes this call"), and stop if anyone objects. Use headphones so the app doesn't capture other people's audio through your microphone.

Privacy#

  • Everything is stored locally under data/ (by default D:\Interview APP\data when installed there).
  • Delete the last few minutes of a transcript at any time during a meeting. Delete whole meetings from the meeting page. Set retention days in Settings to purge old meetings automatically.
  • Local Whisper keeps audio on your PC. The browser fallback (used only when local Whisper isn't installed) sends microphone audio to your browser vendor's speech service (Google for Chrome, Microsoft for Edge).
  • Claude (optional): when enabled, the text of each request, such as an answer transcript or meeting excerpts, is sent to Anthropic's API. Leave the key empty to stay fully local with Ollama.
  • Your API key is stored in data/secrets.json (or read from the ANTHROPIC_API_KEY environment variable) and is never returned by the API or shown in the UI.
  • The server listens on 127.0.0.1 only and rejects requests addressed to other host names.
  • Jobs data: your CV file, profile, saved answers and applications stay in data/. Job searches send only requests for public job listings. When you use Claude for drafting, the redacted CV text and the posting are sent to Anthropic for that request. The browser assistant keeps its own profile in data/browser-profile. Delete that folder to sign out of everything it used.
  • Data-protection laws: running your own job search is personal use (GDPR household exemption; India's DPDP Act section 3(c)(i)). If you adapt this app to process other people's data, you take on a data controller's duties.
